The United States Department of Transportation (DOT) enforces strict regulatory protections governing airline passenger ticketing transactions, operations, and consumer remedies. Under long-standing federal aviation protection rules and updated enforcement mandates, an air carrier's contract of carriage does not grant them total financial impunity during severe operational interruptions. When a commercial airline cancels a scheduled flight for any reason—including severe mechanical failures, scheduling gridlocks, or localized staffing shortages—the passenger is legally entitled to a full cash refund of the ticket price, along with any associated baggage fees or seat assignment upgrades.

A critical point of consumer confusion centers on the difference between cash refunds and promotional airline travel vouchers. Airlines frequently prompt disrupted travelers with automated app notifications offering instant flight credits or digital vouchers to protect their corporate liquidity. However, DOT regulations state that airlines must explicitly notify consumers of their right to a full cash refund before pushing secondary credit alternatives. If a consumer declines alternative routing or travel vouchers following an official cancellation, the airline must issue the refund to the original payment method. If paid by credit card, federal credit law mandates that the credit must be forwarded to the card issuer within seven business days.

These federal refund mandates are triggered not only by absolute flight cancellations but also by what the DOT defines as a "significant schedule change." A significant change occurs if the airline alters departure or arrival times domestically by 3 or more hours, adds un-requested connecting layovers, changes the specific connecting airports for passengers with disabilities, or involuntarily downgrades a passenger to a lower class of service. If any of these core disruptions occur and the consumer chooses not to travel on the modified itinerary, the carrier cannot force them to accept a credit voucher; a clean cash exit remains an absolute legal right.
